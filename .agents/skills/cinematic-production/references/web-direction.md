# Cinematic web direction

Design the film, interaction and reading interface together. The visitor must
understand what the platform is for and reach its useful content without being
forced to watch a fixed-duration opening.

## Spatial presentation

Use one intentional visual language and a shared progress contract. Author
foreground, middle distance and background with clear camera and depth owners.
Effects should reveal a location, an idea or a relationship. Random particles,
independent floating labels and repeated zooms do not establish a spatial story.

Keep the main presentation visually dominant. Place utility and language
controls consistently; keep chapter entrances in the agreed bottom area and
preserve artwork inspection space. Use the actual project's wording and selected
languages. A change of language must preserve progress and composition.

Choose how each segment is rendered before implementation. Photographic footage
can provide realism; live 3D can supply a real camera, depth and interaction;
semantic HTML carries reading, navigation and accessible alternatives. A hybrid
requires an explicit projection, occlusion, timing and loading handoff. Do not
pretend a video plate has live 3D geometry.

## Typography and effects

Select a legible type voice that fits the brief. Give each language real glyph
coverage and sufficient room. Procedural particles may sample exact glyph shapes,
but their representation must share the scene's camera, depth and atmosphere.
Keep an equivalent complete accessible text representation.

Use authored entry, assembly, readable emphasis and disassembly states. Vary
spatial direction and depth intentionally. Check occlusion, near-plane clipping,
focus and emission. A screen-space opacity/scale tween does not fulfill a request
for particle formation in a shared three-dimensional environment.

## Interaction and performance

Native scroll is the source of truth. Derive camera, actors, video time, particle
positions, lights and transitions from the same normalized progress. Stop must
freeze; reverse and fast jumps must reconstruct the same state. Refresh layout
after fonts/media settle and preserve the active composition through rotation.

Budget initial transfer, decoded media, draw calls, pixel ratio, transparency,
shader compilation and memory. Load the current and next required assets; retain
a complete frame or designed poster during decoding. Avoid excessive smoothers,
queued seeks and autonomous loops. Measure performance on a named device/profile.

Retain semantic content, keyboard access, focus, visible controls, reduced motion
and a useful no-WebGL/no-video fallback. Test actual mobile composition, safe
areas, landscape, slow loading and failed resources. Emulated mobile Chromium
does not establish physical Safari or headset behavior.
